Email is still the best way for a farm to stay in touch with the people who buy from it. Social media decides who sees your posts. Email goes straight to the inbox. Here's how to write farm emails people actually open and act on.

## Know who you're writing to

A family that buys a box of produce every week wants something different than a restaurant buying in bulk. If you sell to both, split your list and send each group what they care about: harvest updates and recipes for households, availability and bulk pricing for chefs and stores.

## Write a subject line that earns the open

The subject line decides whether anyone reads the rest. Keep it short and specific, and make it about what's fresh or what's happening now. "Sweet corn is in" or "Last call for Thanksgiving turkeys" beats "October Newsletter" every time.

## Show the farm

Farming is visual, so use it. A good photo of this week's harvest, the new calves, or the crew at work does more than a paragraph. Keep the layout simple so the photos and the main message stand out.

## Tell a short story

Why did you plant this variety? What makes your beef different? What happened on the farm this week? A few honest sentences make people feel connected to where their food comes from, and that's what keeps them buying from you instead of the grocery store.

## Make it personal

Use people's first names. If your email tool can do it, mention what they bought last time or suggest something that goes with it. Most email platforms can do this automatically once it's set up.

## One clear next step

Every email should have one main thing you want people to do: shop this week's harvest, reserve a share, come to the farm day. Make it a big, obvious button with plain words like "Shop now" or "Reserve your share."

## Send it at the right time

Think about when people make decisions. An email about Saturday's market works better on Thursday than on Monday. An email about holiday orders needs to go out weeks ahead, with a reminder before the deadline.

## Check it on a phone

Most people will read your email on their phone. Before you send, send a test to yourself and look at it on your phone. Are the photos loading? Is the button easy to tap?

## Watch what works

Look at your open and click rates after each send. Try two subject lines on a small slice of your list and see which one wins. Over a season, small changes add up.
